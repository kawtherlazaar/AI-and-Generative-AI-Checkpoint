# AI and Generative AI – Checkpoint

### 1. What is AI?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a technology that enables machines and computers to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. Its main purpose is to simulate human abilities such as learning, problem-solving, decision-making, and understanding information. AI helps automate tasks and improve efficiency in different fields.

### 2. How Generative AI Works

Generative AI works by learning patterns and relationships from large amounts of data, including text, images, audio, and videos. During training, AI models learn how information is structured and use statistical methods to predict what comes next. When a user provides a prompt, the model generates new content step by step based on what it has learned. This allows it to create original-looking content that can imitate human creativity.

### 3. Popular Generative AI Models

Several popular Generative AI models are widely used today. GPT-3 and GPT-4, developed by OpenAI, are Large Language Models (LLMs) used for text generation, conversations, and programming. DALL-E generates images from text descriptions, while Stable Diffusion creates and modifies visual content. Gemini is another model that can work with different types of information.

### 4. Limitations of Generative AI

Despite its capabilities Generative AI has several limitations. It can produce biased results because of biases in its training data. It may also generate incorrect information, known as hallucinations. Moreover, its creativity is based on learned patterns rather than human experiences. Other concerns include privacy, misinformation, and the misuse of generated content. Finally, training and operating advanced AI models require significant computational resources and energy.
